# VRP Problem: Solving the Optimization of Cold Chain Logistics Vehicle Scheduling Based on Penguin Optimization Algorithm (Matlab Code Implementation)

1. Overview
Under the influence of the internet, there is an increasing demand for cold chain logistics in agricultural products, yet cost has always been a key factor restricting its development. How to effectively reduce the cost of cold chain logistics has become a hot topic of interest for both national and corporate stakeholders. At the same time, the expansion of the cold chain logistics market and the rising demands for service quality from consumers have compelled companies to enhance their own service quality and improve customer satisfaction to maintain their competitive edge. Therefore, this study aims to provide a basis for the daily dispatch work of cold chain logistics enterprises by optimizing vehicle scheduling and routes while ensuring high levels of satisfaction, thereby achieving the lowest possible overall costs. This research breaks through the limitations of only considering transportation costs and single distribution centers when optimizing cold chain logistics routes, overcoming the shortcomings of traditional genetic algorithms in solving the Vehicle Routing Problem (VRP), and proposes a solution based on the Imperial Penguin Optimization Algorithm for the optimization of cold chain distribution logistics vehicle scheduling. Thus, it holds significant theoretical and practical implications.
2. Imperial Algorithm Optimization
Since Gaurav et al. [4] proposed the Emperor Penguin Optimization (EPO) algorithm in 2018, scholars have conducted research on the initial EPO algorithm and sought further improvements, hoping to apply it to solve various types of optimization problems. Jia et al. [6] based on the EPO algorithm, utilized Masi's entropy as the objective function to propose an improved EPO algorithm. The experimental results prove that the proposed algorithm is more suitable for segmenting high-dimensional complex satellite images. Kumar et al. [7] used a quantum-based multi-objective Emperor Penguin Optimization (MEPSO) algorithm for automatic clustering and applied it to image segmentation. Santos et al. [8] proposed a multi-objective version of the Emperor Penguin Optimization (MPEPO) algorithm and used it for optimal feature selection and cancer classification. Balasubramanian et al. [9] embedded the social engineering optimization meme algorithm into the Emperor Penguin Optimization (EPO) algorithm, enhancing the development capability of the EPO algorithm and successfully classified medical data. Gaurav et al. [7] introduced a new binary Emperor Penguin Optimization (BEPO) algorithm for automatic feature selection. Tang et al. [10] improved the original Emperor Penguin Optimization (EPO) algorithm and proposed an improved EPO algorithm for address building optimization. Gaurav et al. [11] combined the features of multiple objective spot-the-dog algorithms, jellyfish colony algorithm, and EPO algorithms to propose a new hybrid multi-objective heuristic algorithm for solving engineering design problems. Shrivastava [12] applied the Emperor Penguin Optimization (EPO) algorithm to urban traffic management with limited use of radio sensor networks.
